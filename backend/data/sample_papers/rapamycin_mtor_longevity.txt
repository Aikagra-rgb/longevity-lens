TITLE: mTOR Inhibition and Rapamycin Analogues in Longevity Medicine

ABSTRACT
Mechanistic Target of Rapamycin (mTOR) is the central nutrient-sensing kinase governing cellular growth, protein synthesis, and metabolic homeostasis. Hyperactivation of mTOR complex 1 (mTORC1) in nutrient-abundant states inhibits macroautophagy, accelerates cellular senescence, and drives age-related disease. Rapamycin (sirolimus) and its synthetic analogues (rapalogs) are the most robust pharmacological interventions known to extend lifespan across all tested model organisms. This review examines low-dose intermittent rapamycin protocols, their differential effects on mTORC1 versus mTORC2, autophagy induction, and immune function rejuvenation in clinical studies.

1. THE mTORC1 / mTORC2 DUALITY
mTOR functions in two distinct complexes:
- mTORC1: Regulates protein translation (via S6K1 and 4E-BP1), lipid synthesis, and inhibits autophagy (via ULK1 phosphorylation). Chronic overactivation by elevated amino acids, insulin, and glucose suppresses cellular cleanup mechanisms.
- mTORC2: Controls cytoskeletal organization and insulin-mediated Akt activation. Prolonged high-dose rapamycin administration inhibits mTORC2, leading to transient glucose intolerance and immunosuppression.

2. INTERMITTENT DOSING FOR HEALTHSPAN
To capture the longevity benefits of mTORC1 inhibition while avoiding mTORC2 side effects, clinical researchers utilize intermittent weekly dosing (e.g., 3–6 mg once per week).
- Autophagy Induction: Weekly transient inhibition of mTORC1 unleashes autophagy, clearing misfolded protein aggregates and dysfunctional organelles (mitophagy).
- Rejuvenation of Immune Senescence: In phase 2 clinical trials (MANNIK study), low-dose rapalog treatment in elderly adults enhanced response to influenza vaccination by 20% and reduced incidence of respiratory infections, demonstrating immune system rejuvenation rather than immunosuppression.

3. INTERVENTIONAL TESTING PROGRAM (ITP) DATA
The National Institute on Aging (NIA) Interventional Testing Program (ITP) has repeatedly confirmed that rapamycin extends median and maximum lifespan in genetically heterogeneous mice by 9–26%, even when initiated late in middle age (equivalent to 60 human years).

4. BIOMARKER IMPACT & CLINICAL CONSIDERATIONS
- Fasting Glucose & Insulin: Intermittent rapamycin may cause mild, reversible elevations in fasting glucose due to hepatic gluconeogenesis upregulation, accompanied by stable or lowered fasting insulin (indicating preserved peripheral insulin sensitivity).
- Lipid Profile: Transient increases in triglycerides and LDL-C can occur, managed effectively via concurrent Zone 2 exercise and dietary fiber.

CONCLUSION
Intermittent mTORC1 inhibition via low-dose rapamycin represents a premier pharmacological strategy for slowing mammalian biological aging, enhancing immune resilience, and maintaining organ function into late life.
